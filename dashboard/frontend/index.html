Who delivers when the match is on the line?

The 10 batters and 10 bowlers who perform best under pressure, rated only on the deliveries that could actually swing a T20 result.

01 · Definition

What counts as pressure

Not "the last over" or "a big chase" — any ball where the outcome moves the win probability a lot while the match is still in the balance. All three must hold.

CONDITION 1

The ball has high leverage

Expected swing in win probability on this ball vs an average ball. Flagged at LI ≥ 1.5.

CONDITION 2

The match is still live

Batting side's win probability between 15% and 85%. Dead-rubber slogs don't count.

CONDITION 3

Past the free-hit phase

After over 6, or 3+ wickets down, or any ball of a chase.

05 · Method

How the rating is built

Every ball is compared with an average player in the same situation, then corrected for opposition and sample size.

1

Win-probability model

Gradient-boosted model per innings, match-grouped cross-validation, monotone constraints.

2

Leverage Index

Expected swing in win probability over every outcome (0–6, W), normalised so the average ball = 1.

LI = E|ΔWP| ÷ mean E|ΔWP|
3

Context-adjusted value

Compared with the league average in the same innings × 2-over block × wickets × required-rate band. Wickets priced in runs by phase.

4

Opposition adjustment

Ridge regression removes opponent quality — fitted on non-pressure balls only, so pressure can't contaminate its own correction.

5

Empirical-Bayes shrinkage

Small samples pulled toward average by n ÷ (n + k). Bowling shrunk harder (k ≈ 783 vs ≈ 127 balls).

6

Rating & eligibility

Min. 75 pressure balls over 15+ matches. Averaged over full data + 200 bootstraps.

0.8·z(value) + 0.2·z(consistency)
There's no hidden "clutch gene". The best pressure players are excellent players who've proved it in big moments.

Pressure performance is a real but modest signal (split-half ρ ≈ 0.22). The gap between pressure and normal form doesn't repeat (ρ = −0.11 bat, −0.03 bowl), so "clutch lift" gets zero weight.
