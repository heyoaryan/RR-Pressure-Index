System Design

How Pressure Is Defined and Measured

📐
Step 1 — Win Probability Model
Gradient-boosted models (HistGBT) are trained independently for each innings using 5-fold cross-validation grouped by match — so a match never informs its own predictions. Features: runs scored/needed, wickets lost, balls remaining, required run rate, current run rate, IPL flag.

Monotone constraints ensure more runs needed never helps the chase and more wickets never hurts.

Inn 1: AUC 0.679 · Brier 0.226
Inn 2: AUC 0.889 · Brier 0.136
⚡
Step 2 — Leverage Index
For every delivery, we compute the expected absolute change in win probability across all possible outcomes (0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, wicket), weighted by their phase-specific probabilities. This is normalised to a mean of 1.0.

LI = E[|ΔWP|] / mean(E[|ΔWP|])

A ball is high-pressure when: LI ≥ 1.5, win probability between 15–85%, and stage filter (overs > 6, or ≥ 3 wickets down, or second innings).
🎯
Step 3 — Context-Adjusted Value
Each delivery is compared to the league average in an identical context: innings × 2-over block × wickets lost × required-rate band. Runs and wickets are unified via phase-specific run values (5.8 / 7.6 / 4.4 runs per wicket).

Opposition quality is removed via ridge regression fitted on non-pressure balls only — preventing pressure results from contaminating their own adjustment.
📉
Step 4 — Empirical Bayes Shrinkage
Each component is shrunk toward the league average by n / (n + k), where k is estimated from the variance ratio of ball-level noise to between-player variance:

Bat runs: k ≈ 127 balls
Bat wkts: k ≈ 174 balls
Bowl runs: k ≈ 783 balls
Bowl wkts: k ≈ 627 balls

Bowling is shrunk harder because wicket counts are noisier relative to true skill.
🔄
Step 5 — Rating Formula
Rating = 0.8 × z(pressure value) + 0.2 × z(consistency)

Consistency = share of innings/spells with positive net value, shrunk toward 0.50 with prior weight of 12. The rating is computed on full data and 200 bootstrap match resamples; the final score is the average of both.

Weights chosen by out-of-sample testing. "Clutch lift" (pressure minus usual) has zero weight — split-half correlation of −0.11 for batters, −0.03 for bowlers.
✅
Step 6 — Eligibility & Fairness
Minimum: 75 pressure balls, 15 different matches, 100 ordinary balls for a baseline. Leaves 114 batters and 115 bowlers eligible.

Context conditioning (innings × over × wickets × RR band) means a finisher is only compared with other finishers in similar chases. An IPL flag in the win model and opposition regression links the two competitions on the same scale.

Sensitivity checks at LI ≥ 1.0, LI ≥ 2.0, PV-only, stricter eligibility confirm robustness.
